# Patient Guide: Recommended Vaccinations for Adults

## Why Adults Still Need Vaccines

Vaccination is often thought of as primarily a childhood undertaking, but immunity from many childhood vaccines wanes over time, and adults face distinct risks related to age, occupation, travel, chronic health conditions, and pregnancy that childhood vaccination schedules do not address. Staying current with recommended adult vaccines reduces personal risk of serious illness and also contributes to reducing transmission to vulnerable individuals such as infants, older adults, and those who are immunocompromised.

## Influenza Vaccine

Annual influenza vaccination is recommended for all adults, ideally administered before flu season begins since it takes about two weeks after vaccination to develop protective immunity. The vaccine composition is updated yearly to match circulating strains, which is why annual revaccination is necessary rather than a one-time series. High-dose or adjuvanted formulations are available and specifically recommended for adults 65 and older, who tend to mount a weaker immune response to standard-dose vaccines and face higher risk of severe influenza complications.

## Tetanus, Diphtheria, and Pertussis (Tdap/Td)

Adults should receive a Tdap booster once if they did not receive one previously, followed by a Td or Tdap booster every ten years thereafter. Pregnant women are recommended to receive a Tdap dose during each pregnancy, ideally between 27 and 36 weeks gestation, to transfer protective pertussis antibodies to the newborn before their own vaccination series can begin, since pertussis can be particularly dangerous in very young infants.

## Pneumococcal Vaccines

Pneumococcal vaccination is recommended for all adults 65 and older, as well as younger adults with certain chronic conditions such as chronic lung disease, diabetes, or immunocompromising conditions. Current recommendations generally involve one of the newer higher-valency conjugate vaccines, sometimes followed by an additional polysaccharide vaccine depending on which product is used first and the patient's specific risk category, so individualized guidance from a healthcare provider or updated national schedule is important given how frequently these specific recommendations are revised.

## Shingles (Herpes Zoster) Vaccine

The recombinant zoster vaccine is recommended for adults 50 and older, given as a two-dose series, and is also recommended for younger adults 19 and older who are immunocompromised due to their elevated risk of shingles and its complications. This vaccine is recommended even for individuals who have previously had shingles or received the older, less effective live zoster vaccine, since it provides substantially better protection against both shingles itself and the debilitating nerve pain, known as postherpetic neuralgia, that can follow it.

## COVID-19 Vaccination

Recommendations for COVID-19 vaccination continue to evolve as the virus and population immunity change, generally involving updated formulations targeting currently circulating variants, with specific recommendations varying by age, immune status, and time since last dose or infection. Adults should consult current national or regional health authority guidance, as recommended intervals and eligibility for additional doses are updated more frequently than most other adult vaccines.

## Travel and Occupational Vaccines

Certain vaccines are recommended based on specific travel destinations or occupational exposures rather than universally. These include hepatitis A and typhoid vaccines for travel to regions with poor sanitation infrastructure, yellow fever vaccine for travel to certain parts of Africa and South America where it is often a mandatory entry requirement, and hepatitis B vaccine for healthcare workers and others with occupational exposure to blood or bodily fluids. A travel medicine consultation well in advance of departure, ideally four to six weeks, allows adequate time to complete multi-dose series and develop protective immunity before travel.

## Keeping Track of Your Vaccination History

Because adult vaccination schedules involve multiple vaccines with different intervals and eligibility criteria, keeping a personal, up-to-date vaccination record and reviewing it with your healthcare provider at annual visits helps ensure nothing is missed and avoids unnecessary repeat doses when documentation from previous years is otherwise unavailable.
